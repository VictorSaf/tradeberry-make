Range cone

The question

The question

“How far price can go in the next hours, by time of day and the current pace, checked against its own past accuracy.”

04

How it is computed

profile mean squared 1-min return in each of 96 slots, a week of bars, each hour winsorised at median ± 5 MAD
pace fRaw = variance of the last 120 minutes ÷ what the profile expects for them
f = 0.6 × fRaw + 0.4, kept within 0.25–4×
variance Σ over open minutes of (1 + (f − 1) × e^(−m/360)) × slot variance pace fades over ~6 h of trading
bands price × e^(±K σ) K = 1 and 1.96 (session) · k68 and k95 (conformal)
Worked example

EUR/USD at 1.08500, 4-hour σ = 0.21%. 68% range: 1.08500 × e±0.0021 = 1.08272 – 1.08728, a typical move of ±22.8 pips. 95% range: e±1.96 × 0.0021 = 1.08054 – 1.08948. The last two hours moved 1.8× what this time of day usually moves: f = 0.6 × 1.8 + 0.4 = 1.48×, shown in amber (above 1.3× busy).

Did the cone keep its promise?

Every hour of last week, a cone was drawn from what was known then and checked at its horizon. The table compares three models on how often price ended inside each band: Session-aware (this cone), Conformal bands fit on first half, scored on second, and Constant volatility (same data, widening at the same speed every hour). The forecasts overlap, so only about neff = n × 60 ÷ horizon in minutes are independent.

ConditionVerdict
neff under 20Too few independent windows to grade
Inside 95% below 95 − t95, or inside 68% below 68 − t68Too narrow last week: treat its edges as soft
Above 95 + t95, or above 68 + t68Too wide last week: its edges are conservative
Both of the aboveOff target last week
OtherwiseWell calibrated last week (shown in green)
t95 = max(2, 200 × √(0.95 × 0.05 ÷ n_eff)) t68 = max(4, 200 × √(0.68 × 0.32 ÷ n_eff)) two standard errors, in points
Example: 116 hourly forecasts, 4-hour horizon → n_eff 29 → on target within ±8.1 points of 95 and ±17.3 of 68. 93% and 71% inside: well calibrated. In Conformal mode only the newer half is scored (58 forecasts → n_eff 14): too few to grade at 4H.

Your levels against the cone, and the buttons

ColumnMeaning
Distance (σ)Your ticket stop and target, and those of open positions on the pair, in standard deviations from now.
Beyond at endChance price is beyond this level at the end of the horizon.
TouchedChance price touches it at any point before the horizon: 4,000 random paths that follow the cone’s own variance, with a Brownian-bridge correction between 15-minute steps. The draws are fixed for each 15-minute bar.

With both a stop and a target in the ticket: “Within 4H: ticket TP first X%, SL first Y%, neither yet Z%.” Above 50% neither, the trade would most likely still be open at the horizon.

ButtonWhat it puts in the ticket (sends nothing)
Set ticket stop: X (outside 68%)For a buy: ask − the lower 68% edge; for a sell: the upper edge + spread − bid. Rounded outward to 0.1. A move that far happens about one time in three over the horizon.
Set ticket target: X (95% edge) · R:R 1:xFor a buy: the upper 95% edge − ask; for a sell: bid − the lower edge − spread. Never closer than the stop.
Buy limit (68% low) · Sell limit (68% high) · the same at 95%Side, limit type and price. Price ends beyond a 68% edge about one time in 6 per side, a 95% edge about one in 40; it touches them more often.
Use your holding time

Set the horizon to how long you would hold the trade: the cone answers for that time only.

Trust a calibrated cone more

Read the promise check first. Too narrow last week: edges are soft. Too wide: conservative.

Size from the cone

A stop inside the 68% band will often be touched by noise; a target beyond 95% will rarely be reached in this time.

Limits

The cone gives sizes, not direction. Its odds are a past frequency, not a guarantee; the seasonal profile uses the whole week, so the promise check is slightly optimistic. Fat tails and releases can carry price beyond any band.
