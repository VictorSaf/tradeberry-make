The question

The question

“Which live signals have actually paid on this pair. Every signal carries its own track record and a test of whether it beats luck.”

“Every scanner finds signals. This one also checks whether each signal has actually paid on that pair: it finds every past trigger in the last week of 15-minute bars, then measures the move 2 hours later. Triggers never overlap, so the t-statistic is honest. A survivor is a hypothesis to test forward, not a signal.”

The five signals

A trigger counts only on a fresh bar (the signal was not already on). All 16 pairs are scanned, whatever is open; pairs with fewer than 150 closed bars are skipped.

04

How each record is measured

move f = ln(C[i+H] ÷ C[i]) × 10⁴ bp windows across a closed market skipped
score direction × (f − the average move of windows starting in that UTC hour)
squeeze: |f| − the average |f| of that hour
hit share of scores > 0 · base = the same share for every window starting at those hours (the hit to beat, not 50%)
t mean score ÷ (sd ÷ √n) · after a trigger the next H − 1 bars are skipped
stable both halves of the week ≥ 4 cases and the same sign as the whole
spread the pair’s normal spread in bp, paid once (directional signals only)
FDR Benjamini–Hochberg at 10% over every signal × pair with n ≥ 8
05

The verdict per signal and pair

VerdictMeaning, in the tool’s words
▲ Robustt above 2, survives the Benjamini–Hochberg correction for all tests at once, and the same sign in both halves of the week (a weak check: about half of pure-noise results pass it too). The only verdict that counts as a record, and still a hypothesis to test forward.
Significant · unstableSurvives the correction, but the sign flipped between the two halves of the week: not a record.
t < −2 · significantLost on this pair and survives the correction. The tests share currencies and overlap in time: not a contrarian signal.
t > 2 · luck? · t < −2 · luck?Paid (or lost) on this pair in the past week, but does not survive the correction: about as many pass |t| > 2 by chance alone.
NoiseThe past results are within what chance gives.
Too fewFewer than 8 past triggers on this pair: no judgement.
06

The banner, the live list and the report card

PartSays
Banner, no survivorNo signal survives the multiple-testing correction · 0 of N: every “t > 2” below is most likely luck.
Banner, survivors (never green)The count against scans of shuffled data with no order at all: 1 survivor happens in about 1 in 10 scans of pure noise (1 in 13 at 4H); 2 or more in about 1 in 53 (1 in 63 at 4H). The tests are not independent, so the correction is optimistic.
Live signalsSignals firing on the last closed bar, on all 16 pairs, sorted Robust first. Columns: Cases, Hit with its base, Avg (bp and pips, before the spread), After spread, t, Verdict, Details and Open chart. When FX is closed: “Signals at the close”.
DetailsEvery past trigger as a cumulative line, the two half-week averages, and the average move at 15m, 30m, 1h, 2h and 4h: a real edge usually builds or holds with the horizon; noise jumps around.
Signal report cardEach signal pooled over the 16 pairs: cases, hit and base, average bp, a pooled t, clustered (triggers in the same window on several pairs count as one cluster: one market move on five yen pairs is not five pieces of evidence) and on how many pairs it passed t > 2.

Open chart opens the pair. It sets the ticket’s side only for a Robust directional row (“a Robust record, still a hypothesis. Check the cone before you size it.”); otherwise the side is left unchanged. The scan recomputes every 15 minutes of market time.

Colour before numbers

Ignore a live signal whose record is noise, however convincing it looks on the chart. Only Robust is a record.

Count the luck

Compare the passes with the number expected by luck under the table. In line with luck: no signal has shown an edge here.

Let the record call you

Turn the alert on: it stays silent for signals that only look good.

Limits

One week of data. The tests share currencies and overlap in time while the correction assumes independence, so its verdict is optimistic. Hit and average are before the spread. A survivor is a hypothesis to test forward, not a signal.
