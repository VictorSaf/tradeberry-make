I

Orientation

What a backtest is, what this one does, where everything sits.

01

What the Backtester is, and what it is not

A backtest answers one question: if these exact rules had been followed over this stretch of history, with these costs, what would have happened? It does not say what will happen next.

A replay of history

Signals are read on closed bars and filled at the next bar’s open; stops and targets are checked minute by minute on the platform’s 1-minute prices.

With real costs

By default the costs this account pays live: the instrument sheet’s spread (night spread included), swap at every rollover, and the commission. Plus slippage you set.

Honest about luck

Every setting you try is recorded. PSR, DSR and PBO judge the result against what luck alone produces after that many tries.

Why it matters

Almost any idea can be made to look good on one stretch of history by trying enough settings. The Backtester counts your tries and shows the out-of-sample slice, the walk-forward and the luck figures next to the profit, so a pretty curve is not mistaken for an edge.

What it is not

Not a forecast and not advice. A strategy that made money on the last eight weeks can lose on the next eight. The Backtester describes the past under stated assumptions; chapter 18 lists what it cannot model.

Event and state together

The templates combine one event (a cross) with an optional state filter (“close is above EMA slow”). Two events on the same side rarely happen on the same bar, so two crosses give very few trades.

One position at a time

A long signal while already long is ignored (no adding). If both sides’ rules are true on the same bar, neither is taken. With Exit on opposite signal off, an opposite signal while in a trade is ignored too.

Break-even is not free

The stop moves to the entry price, which already includes the spread and slippage paid on entry. A Break-even exit still pays the exit slippage and the commission, so its R is slightly below zero.

Fixed stops near the spread

A fixed stop or trail within 2 × the instrument’s spread is flagged in the form and in the result: “most of it is cost, so exits are mostly noise”.

Account costs

The costs this account pays live: the instrument sheet’s Default spread, or its Night spread between 23:00 and 01:00 server time Monday to Friday, at the moment of each fill; the swap of every 17:00 New York rollover the position is held over (×3 on the triple day); and the sheet’s commission plus the account’s extra commission per lot round turn (Settings). The line under the form spells out the numbers for the chosen instrument. News widening of the spread is live only: the calendar of the past is not kept, so it is not in the backtest, and the app says so.

Where every cost lands

Worked example: cost against a stop

EUR/USD, declared spread 1 pip, slippage 0.2 pip, 15-minute ATR of 6 pips, Stop 2 × ATR = 12 pips (1R). The bid has to fall only 12 − 1 − 0.2 = 10.8 pips to stop the trade (and the fill is 0.2 pip worse again), while a 2R target needs the bid to rise 24 + 1.2 = 25.2 pips. Spread plus entry and exit slippage, 1.4 pips, is 0.117R of every round trip, before commission and swap. On a 5-minute chart with a 4-pip stop the same 1.4 pips would be 0.35R.

Position size, as coded

risk $ = current equity × Risk / trade % ÷ 100
wanted steps = max(the sheet’s minimum, floor(risk $ ÷ (1R in price × quote→USD × contract size × volume step)))
caps: notional ≤ equity × the profile’s leverage · notional ≤ 25 × equity (the per-order cap) · the sheet’s maximum per order
steps = min(wanted, the three caps); lots = steps × volume step
If not even the sheet’s minimum fits under the caps, the signal is skipped and counted. A trade’s R is measured on the risk it actually carried, so capped trades still show true R.
Worked example: size

$100,000 equity, 1% risk = $1,000. EUR/USD stop 12 pips = 0.0012, quote USD, contract 100,000, step 0.01 lot = 1,000 units: wanted = floor(1,000 ÷ (0.0012 × 1,000)) = 833 steps = 8.33 lots. Notional ≈ 8.33 × 100,000 × 1.08 ≈ $900,000, about 9 × equity: under the 25 × cap. Halve the stop to 6 pips and the size doubles to 16.66 lots, ≈ 18 × equity; at 3 pips the 25 × cap would cut it, and the result says “Size capped on N of M entries … those trades risked less than 1% of equity”.

Minimum size

Size never goes below the sheet’s minimum volume. On a very wide stop or a tiny risk %, that minimum can risk more than the percentage asked for; the R of each trade still shows what it really risked.

11

Expectancy, profit factor, drawdown

Three figures carry most of the meaning, and they are linked. With a win rate p, an average win of W R and an average loss of L R:

Expectancy (R per trade) = p × W − (1 − p) × L
Profit factor = p × W ÷ ((1 − p) × L) (the same as Σ wins ÷ Σ losses when every trade risks the same money)
Break-even win rate = L ÷ (W + L)
Drawdown from k full losses in a row = 1 − (1 − L × risk%)ᵏ (size follows equity, as in the Backtester)
Worked example: the template trade-off

40% winners at +2R, losers at −1R: expectancy = 0.4 × 2 − 0.6 × 1 = +0.20R, profit factor = 0.8 ÷ 0.6 = 1.33, break-even win rate = 1 ÷ 3 = 33.3%. Take 0.117R of friction off every trade (the 1.4 pips of chapter 08): wins +1.883R, losses −1.117R, expectancy = +0.083R, profit factor 1.12, break-even win rate 37.2%. Costs took 58% of the edge. Eight losses in a row at 1% risk: 1 − 0.99⁸ = 7.7% drawdown (8.6% with the friction).

+0.08Rexpectancy per trade
Profit factor1.12 Break-even win rate (with friction)37.2% Expected result over the trades, before compounding+8.3% Longest losing streak in at least half of such runs7 losses Drawdown that streak causes−7.6%

Same definitions as the Backtester: a win is P&L > 0, profit factor = Σ wins ÷ Σ losses, expectancy = mean R. Friction is taken off every trade (wins W − f, losses L + f). The streak is exact for independent trades with this win rate: the longest run of losses that half or more of runs of this length contain. Expected result = trades × expectancy × risk %.

Reading the calculator

Set the Backtester’s own Win rate and the average win and loss in R from the trades table, with friction 0 (the Backtester’s R already include costs). The streak line is why the backtest’s Worst streak should not be taken as the worst you will meet: over 100 trades at 40% winners, half of all runs contain 7 losses in a row or more.

The Send to Risk Lab button does this hand-over for you: it opens the Risk Lab with the run’s win rate, the ratio of the average winning R to the average losing R, and the risk %, for a longer projection of outcomes. It needs at least 5 trades including losers.

12

Equity curve and out-of-sample

With Out-of-sample % above 0, the history is cut at bar = floor(n − n × OOS% ÷ 100) of all its bars. Three runs are made with the same settings: the whole history (the headline figures), the part before the cut (In-sample) and the part after it (Out-of-sample). Each starts at $100,000. A trade still open at the cut is closed there in the in-sample run, so the two halves need not add up exactly to the whole.

Line under the tableWhenMeans
“The in-sample result fades out of sample — likely curve-fitted.”IS expectancy > 0 and OOS expectancy < 40% of itWhat looked good on the training part kept less than 40% of its edge on the rest.

The t line appears only when the whole run has at least 20 trades. t = mean R ÷ (sd of R ÷ √n) over the out-of-sample trades.

The slice is out-of-sample once

If you change settings until the out-of-sample row looks good, the slice has become part of the search: the number is no longer an out-of-sample estimate. The walk-forward (chapter 16) repeats the idea properly.

14

How much of this could be luck

Try twenty random strategies and the best of them will look good. This block measures a result against that effect, using every setting you have tried.

The trial record

Every single run, optimizer cell, walk-forward window and all-pairs run is recorded in the account and kept between sessions (up to 500). A try is a template, instrument and timeframe plus everything that changes the trades: active rules and the lengths they use, exits, risk, slippage, costs, leverage and history. Re-running the same setting replaces its entry, so N counts distinct tries. Reset trials clears the record; the toast reminds you the tries still happened.

RowFormula as codedHow to read it
PSRΦ( SR × √(n − 1) ÷ √(1 − skew × SR + (kurt − 1) ÷ 4 × SR²) )Probability the true per-trade Sharpe is above 0, given n trades and the skew and fat tails of their R (Bailey & López de Prado 2012). Needs ≥ 5 trades.
DSRthe same with SR − SR₀ in place of SRProbability the edge is real after N tries: SR₀ is the Sharpe the luckiest of N tries reaches by chance (Bailey & López de Prado 2014). With one try, DSR = PSR.
SR₀√V × ((1 − γ) Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/N) + γ Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/(N e)))V = variance of the per-trade Sharpe across your tries with ≥ 5 trades; γ = 0.5772 (Euler–Mascheroni).
PBOCSCV over 8 time blocks (below)Probability the optimizer’s best cell is a curve fit. ≥ 50% is marked: the optimizer picked noise. Measured only after an Optimizer grid.
N effectivemax(⌈N/2⌉, N − (grid cells − correlated groups))Grid cells whose returns correlate above 0.7 count once; never fewer than half of all tries.
Worked example

60 trades, per-trade Sharpe 0.15, skew 0.8, kurtosis 3.5. PSR = Φ(0.15 × √59 ÷ √(1 − 0.12 + 0.625 × 0.0225)) = Φ(1.219) = 89%. After 40 tries with √V = 0.08: SR₀ = 0.08 × (0.4228 × 1.960 + 0.5772 × 2.358) = 0.175, above this run’s 0.15, so DSR = Φ((0.15 − 0.175) × √59 ÷ 0.9456) = 42%. The Verdict: within what luck gives after 40 tries, no evidence of an edge.

Φ and Φ⁻¹ are the same approximations as the app (Abramowitz & Stegun 7.1.26, Acklam). Kurtosis is the non-excess kind: 3 for a normal distribution. The verdict follows the app’s order (fewer than 20 trades, DSR ≥ 95%, otherwise no evidence), assuming a profit.

PBO: did the optimizer pick noise?

After an Optimizer grid, each cell’s per-bar returns are aligned on their common tail and cut into 8 equal blocks (each at least 10 bars, so at least 80 bars). Every way to choose 4 of the 8 blocks as a training half is tried: 70 splits. In each, the cell with the best Sharpe on the training half is ranked on the other half; its relative rank w = rank ÷ (cells + 1), with ties at mid-rank, gives a logit ln(w ÷ (1 − w)). PBO = the share of splits where the logit is ≤ 0, i.e. the training winner finished at or below the median out of sample. Splits where every cell ties out of sample are left out.

Walk-forward

The honest version of optimisation: tune on a window, trade the next unseen window with the winners, move on. Only the unseen windows count.

ControlChoices (default)What it does
Re-optimise … and …the Optimizer’s parametersThe two parameters tuned in every training window.
byReturn (default) · Profit factor · Sharpe · Expectancy (R)The objective. A cell with fewer than 3 training trades never wins; profit factor is capped at 5.

Window modeRolling window (default) · Anchored (expanding)Rolling: the training window slides. Anchored: it always starts at the beginning and grows.
Window arithmetic, as coded (n bars, W warm-up bars for the largest lengths in the grid, K folds, ratio R)
T = floor((n − W) ÷ (R + K)) bars per test window; fewer than 15 → “Not enough history”
fold k (0-based): train from W + k × T (Rolling) or W (Anchored) to W + (R + k) × T; test the next T bars, the last fold to the end
WF efficiency = (unseen return ÷ unseen bars) ÷ (mean of training return ÷ training bars) × 100%; shown only when the training rate is positive
coin-flip baseline = P(X ≥ windows profitable), X ~ Binomial(profitable windows, ½)
Worked example

8 weeks of EUR/USD 15m ≈ 3,840 bars; sweeping EMA fast × EMA slow, the warm-up is set by EMA slow 100: W = 101. T = floor(3,739 ÷ 8) = 467 bars per test window. Fold 1 trains on bars 101–1,501 and tests 1,502–1,968; fold 5 tests 3,370 to the end (470 bars). If 4 of the 5 test windows are profitable, a coin would do as well 18.8% of the time.

Folds and the baseline

With 3 folds even 3 of 3 profitable windows happen 12.5% of the time on a coin flip, above the 10% bar, so the ▲ verdict cannot appear. With 4, 5 or 6 folds every window must win; with 8 folds, 7 of 8 (3.5%). More folds give the baseline more to work with, at the price of shorter windows.

Pitfalls and limits

Overfitting
PBO ≥ 50%IS fades OOS
Settings tuned until the curve looks good fit the noise of that stretch.
Prefer plateaus, run the walk-forward, and let DSR count every try.
Too few trades
< 20 trades
With 20 trades a per-trade Sharpe of 0.15 has a PSR of only about 75%.
Longer History, lower timeframe, looser rules.
Costs
5m, tight stops
Spread and slippage are fixed in pips; the shorter the stop, the larger the share of R they take.
Keep Account costs; compare Expectancy before and after doubling slippage.
Look-ahead
avoided by design
Rules on closed bars, fills at the next open, ATR of the last closed bar, stops moved for the next minute, stop before target.
Your own choices can still look ahead: picking a template after seeing this week’s chart.
One regime
Market move ±
A few weeks may hold one trend or one range. A trend strategy on a trending stretch looks brilliant.
Compare with Market move; test 12 weeks and several pairs.
The best of many
N tries
Templates, instruments and timeframes searched are part of the selection.
Do not reset trials to make DSR look better: it only hides the search.

What the replay cannot model

News spreads

Spread widening at releases is live only; the backtest uses the Default and Night spreads.

Inside a minute

The order of the high and low within one minute is unknown; the stop is assumed first. Real fills can be better or worse.

Depth and partial fills

Every order fills in full at the modelled price plus slippage, whatever its size.

One position

One trade at a time per run: no adding, no hedging, no portfolio of instruments.

Intrabar drawdown

Equity is marked at bar closes; a deeper dip inside a bar does not show in Max drawdown.

A short past

At most 12 weeks of 1-minute history. That is a sample of a few market conditions, not a cycle.

Glossary

Backtest
A replay of fixed rules over past prices, with stated costs and fill rules.
R
The distance from entry to the initial stop, and the money that distance risks. Results in R compare across sizes and instruments.
Expectancy
The average R per trade, costs included.
Profit factor
Gross profit of the winners ÷ gross loss of the losers.
Drawdown
The fall of equity from its last peak, in % of that peak.
MFE / MAE
Max favourable / adverse excursion: the best and worst open result a trade reached.
Slippage
The difference between the price you aim for and the price you get; here a fixed amount against you per market fill.
In-sample / out-of-sample
The history used to choose the settings / history held back to test them.
Curve fitting
Settings shaped by the noise of one stretch of history; they fail on the next.
Sharpe ratio
Mean return ÷ its standard deviation. Here per trade (on R) or annualised (on per-bar returns).
PSR
Probabilistic Sharpe ratio: probability the true Sharpe is above a bar, given the sample size, skew and kurtosis.
DSR
Deflated Sharpe ratio: the PSR with the bar raised to what the best of N tries reaches by luck.
PBO
Probability of backtest overfitting, by combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV).
Skew, kurtosis
Lopsidedness and tail weight of the R distribution; kurtosis 3 = normal tails.
Walk-forward
Repeated tune-then-test on consecutive windows, keeping only the test windows.
WF efficiency
Unseen return per bar as a share of training return per bar.
Warm-up
The first bars, used only to give the indicators enough data.
