Market Brief

An analyst-style reading of one instrument, generated on your device from the terminal’s prices: trend, momentum, volatility, key levels, upcoming events, your own positions, and a trade plan built by fixed rules. This guide explains every part of the page, how each number is calculated, how to read it and how to use it.

I

Orientation

What the brief is, what to read first, where everything is.

01

What the brief is, and what it is not

The brief applies one set of rules to any instrument and writes down what it finds. Three properties decide how you should use it.

Explainable

Every sentence is backed by a number shown on the page. Hover or focus any highlighted number for its explanation. No hidden model.

Descriptive

It describes what price has done. “Strong down” means the recent 4H bars fell steadily, not that the next bar will fall.

A structure, not a signal

The plan fixes entry, stop and target before you trade, so the decision is made calmly. Taking the trade is your call.

Not financial advice

The brief only reads price. It does not know the news behind a move, other traders’ positioning, or anything that is not in the chart. Past trend does not predict the next move.

The header says where the numbers come from: from platform prices is the broker platform’s live feed. Events come from the terminal’s Calendar; if it is unavailable the brief writes (calendar unavailable) next to any event it mentions.

02

The brief in one minute

Short on time? Read these five things, in this order.

Verdict · Trend 4H

Trend or not, which way, how strong. Beyond ±55 strong, within ±20 none.

Timeframes

3/3 = all agree. Less in a trend usually means a pullback.

Event risk

A high-impact release soon can override everything else.

Key levels

How far R1 and S1 are, compared with the hourly ATR.

Plan template

Entry, stop, target, reward : risk, money at risk.

Already in a position?

Read the last paragraph of the Reading first: it tells you whether your positions point with or against the 4H trend, and reminds you to check every stop.

06

The five headline figures

The strip under the header summarises the whole brief. Each cell has a full explanation on hover.

Trend 4H: the headline

From −100 to +100 on 4-hour bars. Where it falls on this scale is the verdict:

Timeframes

The same trend score on 15m, 1H and 4H. The cell counts how many point the same way as the 4H trend with a score beyond ±20 (0/3 when 4H itself is flat). Chapter 08 shows the three typical patterns.

Momentum · RSI 1H

The 14-period Relative Strength Index on 1-hour bars: the average up-move against the average down-move of the last 14 hours, from 0 to 100.

Pressure, not turning points

In a strong trend RSI can stay beyond 70 or below 30 for a long time. With the trend, an RSI back near 50 is usually a calmer entry than one at an extreme. An extreme against your position is a reason to review the plan, not a signal by itself.

Volatility · ATR 1H

The big number is a ratio: the hourly ATR now ÷ the ATR at the same hours of the day earlier in the week. Under it, the hourly ATR itself (Wilder, 14 complete hours) in pips for FX or points otherwise: how far one hour moves on average.

Event risk

The next scheduled release in the next 6 hours for the currencies that move the instrument: both currencies of an FX pair, otherwise its quote currency (USD for a US index, EUR for the DAX).

High impact

Around a high-impact release price can jump either way, and spreads and slippage widen for a few minutes. Avoid opening right before one, or size down and accept the wider spread.

07

How the trend score works

The score is two simple measurements, weighted and added. The Why panel on the page shows both parts for the instrument you are looking at.

Try it: build a score

Set the two checks and the slope, and watch the score and its label change. This is the exact arithmetic the brief uses.

−2−1.20+1.2+2
+78▲ Strong up
Position · 55%+55Slope · 45%+23Total+78

Slope points = slope ÷ 1.2, capped at ±1, × 45. Position points = (check 1 + check 2) ÷ 2 × 55.

How to read the mix

Pattern in the Why panelWhat it usually means
Full position, small slopeThe averages are still stacked but the move is losing pace: the trend may be slowing.
Big slope, position 0The EMA20 is turning fast while price and the averages have not lined up yet: often a fresh turn.
Both full (±100)A mature, steady move. Good context for trend trades; check whether price is stretched (RSI, 24h range).
Position and slope oppositePrice is crossing its averages: transition. The score sits near zero.
Worked example

A crypto pair shows −100. Price is below the EMA20 (−1) and the EMA20 below the EMA50 (−1): position −2 of ±2 → −55. The EMA20 fell 1.24 ATR in 5 bars, more than 1.2 → −45. Total −100, strong down.

The table under the Why panel repeats the calculation on 15m, 1H and 4H, with ✓ / ✗ for “price above EMA20” and “EMA20 above EMA50”, the score and the RSI. Short timeframes turn first. Three patterns cover most cases:

Timing an entry

When 15m and 1H turn back in the 4H direction after a pullback, the trend has resumed on the timeframes you enter on. When 15m alone turns against a 4H trend, it is the first sign of a pullback, nothing more.

11

Key levels

Prices where the market turned or paused recently. Reactions are most likely there, so plans place entries, stops and targets around them.

Strength, dot by dot

SourceAdds
Each swing touch● 1
24h high or low● 1
Round number◐ ½

Five dots is the most shown. More dots: respected more often, watched by more traders. One or half a dot: a weak reference, a checkpoint rather than a wall.

Distance vs ATR

Compare a level’s distance with the hourly ATR: a level less than one ATR away can be reached within the hour.

How to use levels

Buyers

Take profit or tighten the stop near R1. Avoid buying right under a strong resistance.

Sellers

Take profit or tighten the stop near S1. Avoid selling right above a strong support.

Stops

Beyond a strong level, with room for normal swings. Never exactly on it: levels are zones.

Breaks

A clean break often flips a level: broken resistance can act as support later, and the reverse.

Size, risk, reward

Reward : risk is the distance to the target divided by the distance to the stop. It decides how often you must win just to break even:

Reward : riskRisk vs rewardBreak-even win rate
1 : 1
50%
1 : 1.5 template minimum
40%
1 : 2
33%
1 : 3
25%

Red = risk (left), green = reward (right). Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + reward/risk), before costs.

Ratio is not probability

A high ratio makes a trade worth taking; it does not make it more likely to work. Far targets are reached less often.

Money at risk

Under the plan, type a size (lots, or shares for stocks; it starts from the ticket’s size). The line below shows the loss at the stop in dollars and as a share of your equity, and the gain at the target.

Size from the loss, not the feeling

Many traders keep the risk per trade small and constant, often 0.5–1% of equity. Change the size until the risk line says so, then load the plan.

Practice

Putting it together before, during and after a trade.

15

A routine before a trade

  1. Choose
    Pick the instrumentScan for clear trends, or the exception in its group, and click it.
  2. Choose
    RefreshMake sure the brief is fresh.
  3. Context
    Trend and timeframesWith the trend, against it, or in a range?
  4. Context
    Why panelAccelerating (slope) or only stacked (position)?
  5. Risk
    EventsA high-impact release close by: wait, or reduce size.
  6. Structure
    LevelsWhere R1 and S1 are, in ATRs. Look at them on the 1H chart.
  7. Plan
    Template and sizeRead reward : risk and the steps; type the size until the risk is the share you accept.
  8. Decide
    Load and reviewLoad into the ticket, read its warnings, risk and margin, change what you disagree with, then send or skip.
  9. Record
    Copy into the journalSo you can compare what you expected with what happened.
  10. Wait
    AlertsIf the entry is not there yet, set alerts at the key levels and stop watching the screen.

17

Limits and pitfalls

It describes the past

−100 says the recent bars fell steadily. Markets often reverse right after strong moves; the brief cannot know when.

Only price

No news, fundamentals or order flow. A headline can override every number on the page.

Averages lag

The score turns after price. Fresh turns show first in the slope and on short timeframes.

Levels are zones

Price can overshoot by a few pips or points and still respect a level. Stops exactly on it get hit.

Thin history

Too few 4H bars: “Not enough 4H history for a score yet”, and the score stays flat.

Costs are extra

Spread, commission and swap are not in the plan. The ticket shows them; check before sending.

Closed markets

When a market is closed, the numbers describe the last session, and spreads at the reopen can be wider than the plan assumes.

19

Glossary

ATR
Average true range: the average size of one bar’s move, gaps included. The brief uses Wilder’s 14-period ATR.
EMA
Exponential moving average: the average of the last N closes, weighted toward recent ones. EMA20 is fast, EMA50 slow.
RSI
Relative strength index: average up-move against average down-move over 14 bars, 0–100.
Pip / point
The unit of distance: pips for FX (0.0001, or 0.01 on JPY pairs), points for other instruments.
Swing high / low
A bar whose high (low) is the highest (lowest) of the bars around it: a turn in price.
Resistance / support
A level above price where rallies stopped before / below price where declines stopped before.
Pullback
A move against the main trend that has not ended it.
Limit order
An order that fills only at the entry price or better.
Reward : risk
Distance to the target ÷ distance to the stop.
Basis point
0.01%. Used for currency strength.
G8 currencies
USD, EUR, JPY, GBP, CHF, AUD, CAD, NZD. The 16 FX majors are pairs of them.
Equity
Balance plus the profit or loss of open positions.
