Calculators

Three small calculators that turn a plan into numbers before you trade: how many lots a stop and a risk allow, what one pip or point of a position is worth and how much margin it ties up, and where a steady monthly return leads. They use the same instrument sheet, the same live conversion rates and the same leverage rules as the order ticket.

I

Orientation

What each calculator answers and where everything is.

01

What the calculators are

The Calculators tab holds three cards side by side. Each one answers a single question with the account rules of tradeBerry, so the numbers match what the ticket and the account would show for the same trade.

Position size

“How big can I trade if I risk this share of my account and my stop is this far away?” It gives the lots, the money really at risk, the pip value and the margin.

Pip / point value & margin

“What is one pip (or point) of this size worth, and what does it cost to hold?” It gives the pip value, the units, the margin, the notional and the spread cost.

Compounding

“Where does a fixed monthly return lead after so many months?” It gives the final balance, the gain, the total return and the annualised rate.

Nothing is sent

The calculators never place, change or check an order. They read the instrument sheet, the live quotes and your leverage profile, and only display results. The Account field is whatever you type: it is not linked to your balance.

Strategy Lab is opened per user: the administrator ticks it in Administration. If you do not see the Strategy Lab page, it is not enabled for your login.

II

The instrument behind the numbers

The four facts every result is built from: the unit of a move, the contract size, the conversion into dollars and the leverage.

04

Pips and points

Stops, targets, spreads and pip values are counted in one displayed unit per instrument:

  • FX: the pip. The pip is one step of the second-to-last digit when the price has an odd number of decimals (5 on EUR/USD, 3 on USD/JPY), else the last digit. So 0.0001 on EUR/USD, 0.01 on USD/JPY and EUR/HUF.
  • Everything else: the point (pt), the platform’s smallest price step, 10−digits. That is 0.01 on US.500, GOLD, BTCUSD and stocks, 0.1 on DE.30, 1 on JAP225. It is not the “index point” of everyday speech.
The stop starts in the right unit

When you pick an instrument, the Stop field is filled with New order’s default stop for it: the larger of 3 × the spread (never below the sheet’s default spread) and a volatility distance (2 × the instrument’s typical one-hour move, or half the 1-hour ATR(14)), rounded up, in pips or pt. At the sheet’s snapshot that is about 12 pips on EUR/USD, 1,835 pt (18.35 index points) on US.500 and 1,749 pt ($17.49) on GOLD. It is a starting point, not your stop: put the stop where your idea is wrong. Remember that a CFD pt is the last digit of the price, so 25 pt on US.500 would be only 0.25 index points.

06

From quote currency to USD

Profit and loss on any instrument is paid in its quote currency: JPY on USD/JPY, GBP on EUR/GBP, EUR on DE.30. The account is in USD, so every money figure goes through one conversion: the value of one unit of that currency in USD, read from the live quotes.

Two values are converted:

  • The quote currency, for the pip value, P/L and the spread cost of every instrument.
  • The base currency, for the notional of an FX position (100,000 EUR in USD on EUR/USD). A CFD’s notional uses its price times the quote currency instead.
Example

EUR/GBP: one pip on one lot is 100,000 × 0.0001 = 10 GBP. GBP in USD is the GBPUSD bid, 1.3377, so the pip value is $13.38. Its notional is 100,000 EUR × 1.12233 (the EURUSD bid) = $112,233.00.

Because these are live bids, the pip value of a non-USD quote currency moves a little all day. The calculators re-read them every 2 seconds while open.

08

Position size

You choose how much you are willing to lose and where the idea is wrong; the calculator gives the largest size that keeps the loss at the stop within that amount.

risk money = Account × Risk % ÷ 100
pip value per lot = contract size × pip (or pt) × (1 quote currency in USD)
lots = ⌊ risk money ÷ (Stop × pip value per lot) ÷ step ⌋ × step
Amount at risk = lots × Stop × pip value per lot
Pip value = pip value per lot × lots
Margin at profile = notional(lots) ÷ effective leverage
⌊ ⌋ rounds down to the sheet’s volume step (0.01 lots on FX, 1 share, 100 on XRPUSD). Every line below the size is computed from the size shown, not from the exact quotient.

Step by step: USD/JPY

Risk money = 100,000 × 1 ÷ 100 = $1,000. Pip value per lot = 100,000 × 0.01 × (1 ÷ 158.000) = $6.3291. Exact size = 1,000 ÷ (25 × 6.3291) = 6.32 lots, already on the 0.01 step. Amount at risk = 6.32 × 25 × 6.3291 = $1,000.00. Notional = 632,000 USD = $632,000, so margin at 1:500 = $1,264.00.

When the size cannot be traded

The size is shown even when the ticket would refuse it, with a red line saying why. Three checks, the same as the order limits of the account:

CaseInputsResult
Below the minimumGOLD · 1000 · 0.5% · 2000 pt ($20 move)Exact 0.0025 lots → 0 lots, $0.00 at risk, and “Below the sheet's minimum: Minimum size is 0.01 lots — this risk and stop allow no position.”
Above the maximumBTCUSD · 10000 · 1% · 25 pt (a stop typed as if it were pips)400 lots; “Maximum size is 10 lots per order: an order this size is refused.”, and the notional line: $32,547,640.00 above $250,000.00.
Notional capUS.500 · 10000 · 1% · 25 pt (0.25 index points)8 lots, within the maximum of 100, but a notional of $2,996,500.00: “Notional $2,996,500.00 is above the per-order cap of 25× equity ($250,000.00): an order this size is refused.”
What a red line means

The stop is too tight or the risk too large for that instrument (a size above the limits), or too small (below the minimum). Widen or tighten the stop where the idea really is, change the risk, or trade something else; do not just shrink the stop to make a number fit.

The margin check against your free margin is not part of this card: the ticket does it when you send the order.

10

Pip / point value & margin

For a size you already have in mind: what one pip or pt is worth, how much of the asset it is, what it costs in margin and in spread.

Pip value = Lots × contract size × pip (or pt) × (1 quote currency in USD)
Notional = Lots × contract size × (1 base currency in USD) FX
Notional = Lots × contract size × bid × (1 quote currency in USD) every other instrument
Margin = Notional ÷ Leverage
Spread cost = live spread (in pips or pt) × Pip value
The label reads “Point value” instead of “Pip value” on instruments counted in pt.

Why DE.30 shows euros and dollars

Units is in the instrument’s own currency (€2.50 per pt for a lot). Point value is in USD: €2.50 × 1.12233 = $2.81.

11

Compounding

The same monthly return applied to a growing balance:

Final balance = Start × (1 + r)Months, r = Monthly return % ÷ 100
Total gain = Final balance − Start
Total return = (Final balance ÷ Start − 1) × 100
Annualised = ((1 + r)12 − 1) × 100
Annualised depends on the monthly return only, not on Months. Gains show a + sign, losses a − sign; percentages have one decimal.

Inputs are checked first, in red: “Start must be above 0.”, “Monthly return must be above −100%.”, “Months must be a whole number, 1 or more.”

Start · monthly · monthsFinal balanceTotal gainTotal returnAnnualised

The ▲ / ▼ marks are this guide’s; the card shows the + or − sign.

Read it as arithmetic, not as a plan

Real monthly results vary, and losses compound too: −3% a month halves an account in two years. A steady figure above a few percent a month is rare for long; the Risk Lab’s Monte Carlo test shows the spread of outcomes a strategy can really have.

Same risk, very different notional

$100 at risk is $11,000 of Apple shares, $45,000 of EUR/USD or $145,000 of EUR/HUF. The notional depends on how far the stop is in money, not on the risk.

Non-USD quotes change the pip value

USD/JPY pays $6.33 a pip per lot, EUR/GBP $13.38. The same 25-pip stop gives 0.63 and 0.29 lots for the same $100.

Rounding bites on small sizes

On DE.30 the exact 0.1188 lots rounds down to 0.11: $92.59 at risk instead of $100. The smaller the account, the larger the share lost to the step.

16

Using them in practice

Stop first, size second

Put the stop where the trade idea is wrong, on the chart. Then let Position size give the lots; do not move the stop to reach a size.

Type your real equity

The Account field is not linked to your balance. Use the equity in the account bar, not the default 100000.

Check the stop’s unit on CFDs

Convert the price move to pt (chapter 13). A red “Maximum size” or “Notional” line usually means a CFD stop was typed as if it were pips.

Check the spread against the stop

Spread cost ÷ risk money is the share of your risk paid on entry. A 1.2-pip spread on a 25-pip stop is about 5%; on a 5-pip stop it is 24%.

Size correlated trades as one

Two positions that move together are one bet: split the risk between them. See the Correlation guide.

Compare regimes before you choose one

Switch the jurisdiction in Settings and watch Margin at profile: the size stays, the margin changes.

A routine before a trade

  1. Plan
    Entry and stop on the chartThe distance in pips, or in price for a CFD.
  2. Convert
    Stop in the right unitPips on FX; price move ÷ pt on everything else.
  3. Size
    Position size cardYour equity, your risk %, the stop. Read the lots and the amount at risk.
  4. Check
    No red lines; margin fitsMargin at profile against the free margin in the account bar.
  5. Cost
    Pip / point value cardThe spread cost for that size, and the value of each pip or pt.
  6. Trade
    The ticketType the size and the stop; the ticket runs the same rules again before sending.

17

Limits and caveats

The stop is a price, not a promise

Amount at risk assumes the stop fills exactly at its level. Gaps, a weekend or fast news can fill it further away, and the loss is then larger.

Costs not included

Position size ignores spread, commission and swap. Pip / point value shows the spread cost separately; commission and swap are not in either card.

Rates move

Pip values on non-USD quotes and every notional follow the live bids; a figure read a minute ago can differ slightly.

Margin checked at the ticket

The cards do not compare the margin with your free margin; the ticket does, when the order is sent.

No prices, no figures

Without a live quote for a rate the conversion needs, money figures show —.

Compounding is constant

One return every month, no costs, no drawdowns. It shows arithmetic, not what a strategy will do.

Not advice

The calculators size and price a trade you have decided; they do not say whether to take it. Nothing here is financial advice.

Glossary

Pip
The unit of an FX price move: 0.0001 on most pairs, 0.01 on JPY pairs and some exotic pairs.
Point (pt)
The unit of a move on every non-FX instrument: the smallest price step, 10−digits. Not the same as an index point.
Lot
The unit of size. Its contract size is on the instrument sheet: 100,000 of the base currency on FX, 100 Ounces on GOLD, $50 per 1.00 on US.500, one share on a stock.
Base / quote currency
In EUR/GBP, EUR is the base (what you buy or sell), GBP the quote (what P/L is paid in).
Pip value / point value
The USD value of a one-pip (or one-pt) move for a given size.
Notional
The full USD value of a position, before leverage.
Margin
The part of the notional the account sets aside while the position is open: notional ÷ leverage.
Leverage
Notional ÷ margin, written 1:30, 1:500 … Higher leverage needs less margin for the same position.
Jurisdiction cap
An optional retail leverage limit per margin class (ESMA for EU, UK and AU; NFA for the US), set in Settings.
Volume step
The increment sizes must follow (0.01 lots, 1 share, 100 on XRPUSD). Position size rounds down to it.
Amount at risk
The loss if the stop fills at its level, for the size shown.
Spread cost
What the bid-ask spread costs on entry for a size: spread × pip value.
Compounding
Earning a return on earlier returns: the balance grows by the same percentage of a larger amount each month.
