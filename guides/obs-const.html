Constellation

Use it before you add a position: it tells you whether the new trade is really new, or the same bet you already hold under another name. It always covers the 16 G8 pairs; CFDs and other FX pairs have no node.

The question

The question

“Which pairs move together right now. A tree of the strongest correlations between the 16 pairs: adjacent = directly correlated; further along a branch is not implied.”

What you see

03

Controls

ControlOptions · defaultEffect
Window4H · 1D · 3DThe 1-minute returns of the last 4 hours, 24 hours or 3 days of trading. 4H is the most recent picture and the noisiest; 3D the steadiest. Shared with the Regime map.
returns1m · 5m · 15mThe step of the price changes compared. 5m and 15m sum 1-minute returns over complete slots of the UTC clock: a fifth or a fifteenth as many returns, so ρ is noisier on short windows. Not kept after a reload.

The right-hand cards, in order

CardWhat it saysRule
Hubs — pairs with the most linksPills with each hub’s link count; click to open. Under them, tree stability: S of 15 links also in the previous 1D.Hub: links ≥ the 3rd-highest count and ≥ 2. Stability ≥ 12: “a settled structure”; fewer: “the tree is reshuffling, so read single links loosely”. Needs a full previous window (3D usually has none).
Unusual links nowPills A–B broke|new normal → now, the strongest first, up to 8 (+N more).|Δz| ÷ SE > 3 (Fisher z, widened ×k). Broke = weaker than normal, new = stronger. Needs 30 returns per pair now and a history of at least 1.5 windows.
Tightest linkThe tree link with the largest |ρ|, in words.|ρ| ≥ 0.7 “move almost as one” (highlighted) · ≥ 0.4 “share part of their moves” · below “only weakly linked”.
Least linked to your positionsIf you hold one of the 16: the unheld pair whose strongest link to your positions is weakest.A candidate diversifier, not a recommendation.
Most independent pairThe pair furthest from its nearest neighbour, with d.0 = moves as one, 1.41 = unrelated. Pairs that did not move are left out, never called independent.
Your positionsNet lots per pair; every held pair-pair with |ρ| > 0.7, judged.Held so they win and lose together: concentrated risk. Held so one offsets the other: a hedge, not added risk.
Your currency exposureNet USD notional per currency, as in the Exposure panel; currencies of a hub pair are marked.A pair is long its base currency and short its quote. Currencies under $1 net are left out.
Return intervalMean |ρ| of the 120 links at 1, 5 and 15 minutes, with n.Within 0.05: “no Epps effect here”. Under 30 returns: “Thin data … read the tree loosely.”
06

How to read it

You seeIt means
A cluster of circles joined by thick linesOne trade expressed several ways. Two positions in it are rarely independent.
A thick blue lineAn inverse link, usually from a shared currency on opposite sides (EUR base in one pair, USD base in the other). Buying both is a partial hedge; buying one and selling the other doubles the exposure.
A link on 4H only, not on 1D or 3DPossibly a passing coincidence. A link on all three windows is real structure.
One unusual-link markMost likely a sampling swing: with 120 links checked, about one window in four still shows a mark by chance alone.
07

How to use it

Before adding a trade

Open the pair you want to trade (amber ring) and look at its neighbours. A position on one of them is already part of this trade.

Size correlated trades as one

If Your positions says concentrated risk, their stops are likely to be hit together. Pick the one with the better setup or the lower spread.

Check across windows

Switch 4H, 1D, 3D before relying on a link. For a trade held for hours look at 4H and 1D; for days, at 3D. Use 15m with 3D to check a link holds for slower trading.

Limits

Correlation is measured, not explained: the tree does not say why two pairs move together or that one moves the other. Circle colour always shows the last 4 hours, even on 1D and 3D. Links between the 16 pairs and any other instrument are not shown: use Correlation (the next tab in Research) for those.
