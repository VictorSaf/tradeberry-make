I

Orientation

What the table measures, what to read first, where everything is.

01

What the table is, and what it is not

Each row is one instrument. Its numbers answer three questions: how far does it move in an hour, is that more or less than usual for these hours, and where is price within today’s range.

How far, not which way

Volatility measures the size of the moves. A busy market can go up or down; nothing on this page predicts direction.

Comparable across classes

ATR as a % of price puts a JPY pair, a stock, an index and bitcoin on one scale; pips or points give the distance your stop is measured in.

Against its own hours

“vs week” compares now with the same hours of the day earlier in the week, so a busy London afternoon is judged against other afternoons, not against quiet nights.

Why it matters

A 20-pip stop is generous on EUR/GBP and tight on GBP/JPY. Without knowing how far an instrument moves in an hour, a stop is a guess: too close and ordinary noise takes you out, too far and the position is too big for its risk.

II

Reading it

Every column: what it measures, how it is computed, how to read it.

04

ATR: one hour of movement

ATR (average true range) is the average size of one hour’s move, gaps included. It is the same hourly ATR every panel of the terminal shows (the Market Brief, the ticket’s ATR stops).

How it is computed

  1. Take the last complete one-hour bars (up to 101; the hour in progress is left out).
  2. For each bar, its true range (Fig. 2).
  3. Average them with Wilder’s smoothing over 14 hours: each new hour moves the average by 1/14 of the difference.
  4. Show it in pips (FX) or points (other instruments), and as % of price.

An instrument with fewer than 15 complete hours of history has no row yet.

Example

EUR/GBP shows 6.9 pips (0.08%), AUD/JPY 29.5 pips (0.27%). In the same hour AUD/JPY usually moves more than three times as far, in % terms: a stop that fits one is wrong for the other.

05

vs week: busy or quiet?

The ATR now divided by the normal for these hours: the average true range of the same hours of the day in the earlier part of the history (the hours covered by the last 14 bars, matched by UTC hour, at least 10 of them).

vs weekMeansWhat to do
> 1.25×Moving more than usual for these hours: news, a breakout, a busy session.Same plan, smaller size: the stop in pips must be wider, the money at risk should not be.

< 0.8×Quieter than usual.Expect less: closer targets, or wait. Quiet tends not to last, but that says nothing about which way it ends.

06

The 24h range

The high minus the low of the bars in the last 24 hours of clock time (a market closed for longer: its last bar), shown three ways:

FormExampleRead it as
Pips / points54pHow far it travelled from low to high.
% of price0.50%The same, comparable across instruments.
× one hour of ATR1.8×How many “typical hours” the day’s range spans.
After a weekend or a closed session

The window is 24 hours of the clock. On Monday morning it holds only the hours since the market reopened, so the range (and its × ATR) is small. Read it as “the range so far”.

07

Price in the 24h range

At an end

Price is testing the day’s extreme. Both breakouts and reversals start there: wait for the break to hold or for a clear rejection before acting.

In the middle

Room on both sides. A trade from here has the day’s range to work in, and the stop can sit beyond the nearer end.

With the ATR

Distance to an end in pips ÷ ATR = how many typical hours of movement until price reaches it.

III

Using it

Turning one hour of movement into stops, size and realistic expectations.

09

How far price travels in time

Typical movement does not grow in a straight line with time. For a market that moves at random, the usual distance grows with the square root of time: four hours move about twice as far as one, not four times.

Holding timeTypical moveEUR/USD at 14 pips ATR
1 hour1 × ATR≈ 14 pips
4 hours2 × ATR≈ 28 pips
9 hours3 × ATR≈ 42 pips
1 day of trading≈ 4.9 × ATR≈ 69 pips
Reverse it for targets

A target k ATR away needs about k² typical hours: a 3-ATR target is a 9-hour idea, not a 1-hour one. If your holding time is short, a far target is wishful.

10

Stops and size from ATR

A stop inside one hour of ATR is within ordinary noise: it is likely to be hit by movement that means nothing. The usual approach: place the stop at a multiple of ATR beyond your level, then size the position so that stop costs a fixed share of your equity.

0.48lots
Stop distance21.0 pipsMoney at risk$100Typical hours to reach the stop by noise alone≈ 2.3 h

lots = equity × risk% ÷ (stop pips × pip value per lot). Pip value is about $10 per lot on pairs quoted in USD; the ticket shows the exact value for each instrument. Hours ≈ (stop ÷ ATR)², the square-root rule.

The point

When volatility doubles, the stop in pips doubles and the size halves: the money at risk stays the same. That is how a busy market (vs week above 1.25×) is traded without taking more risk.

11

What to do with it

Choose what to trade

Short-term trading needs movement: sort by Volatility. Wide-stop swing trades on calm instruments keep size reasonable.

Set the stop

Beyond your level and outside one hour of ATR. In pips from the Pips / pt column.

Set the size

From the stop and your risk per trade (chapter 10), not from conviction.

Check the target

Is it reachable in your holding time? Use the square-root rule (chapter 9).

Read the regime

Market vs its week above 1.25×: reduce size across the board. Below 0.8×: expect less, be patient.

Watch the ends

Sort At an end to find instruments at the 24h high or low, then wait for the break or the rejection.

A routine before a trade

13

Limits and pitfalls

It looks back

ATR is an average of past hours. A release or a headline can produce a move several times the ATR in minutes.

Gaps are bigger than hours

A weekend or session gap can jump past any stop; ATR includes gaps only as they happened in the history.

Rules of thumb

The square-root rule assumes random movement; trending days travel further, ranging days less.

Not direction

High volatility is not a buy or sell signal. Nothing here is a forecast or financial advice.

15

Glossary

ATR
Average true range: the average size of one bar’s move, gaps included (Wilder, 14 bars). Here one bar = one hour.
True range
The largest of the bar’s high − low and its distances from the previous close.
Pip / point
The unit of distance: pips for FX (0.0001, or 0.01 on JPY pairs), points for other instruments.
Volatility
How much price moves, whatever the direction.
Regime
Whether the market is busier, normal or quieter than usual for the time of day.
Square-root rule
For random movement, the typical distance over k hours ≈ √k times the distance over one hour.
Risk per trade
The share of equity lost if the stop is hit.
Pip value
The money one pip is worth for one lot; it depends on the instrument and the account currency.
