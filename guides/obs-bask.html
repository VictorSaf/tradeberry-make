Baskets

Use a basket when your view is on a group, for example commodity currencies against safe havens. Read the index like a price: up means the long side outperformed the short side. The right panel has two views: Analyse first, then Trade. Nothing is sent until you confirm in the Trade view.

05

Analyse: stretch, alerts, behaviour

CardComputedRead it as
Stretchz = (ln close − ln EMA20) ÷ σ of those gaps, on the chosen timeframe (needs 60 candles). Over 3 days: how many moves past 2σ came back to the mean within 20 bars, against 20 shuffled copies of the same moves.Beyond ±2σ the index is stretched. Check the random-walk comparison before treating it as a reversal: the EMA20 lags, so it catches up with price even if price stops moving. Not a forecast.
Index alertsAlert at ± N bp from now (1–2000).A toast and a chime when the basket moves that far either way; then the alert is removed.
BehaviourDaily volatility = SD of the last 1,440 one-minute index returns × √1440. Proxy = the G8 pair with the largest |ρ| to the index over the last day; β = cov ÷ var.|ρ| under 0.8: “no single pair captures it, which is the point of a basket.” Otherwise the pair follows ρ² of the index; Trade <pair> instead loads it sized on β (not sent).

Basket overlap: above ρ 0.7 “holding both is largely the same bet”; below −0.7 “they move opposite: holding both the same way largely hedges one with the other.” By hour: a few samples per hour, so neighbouring hours are not reliably different.

Trade: one ticket for all legs

SettingDefault · rule
ModeRisk % (0.05–5, default 0.5% of equity) · Size (0.1–20 × $100k notional, default 1)
Stop · TargetAuto: stop = max(5, ½ × daily volatility) bp; target = 1.5 × stop. Once you type a value it stays a number (0 = none).
Trail · Break-even atOff by default, in bp of the index.
EntryMarket · Limit (on a pullback of N bp) · Stop entry (on a breakout of N bp), default 15 bp
Worked example

Daily volatility 48 bp: auto stop 24 bp, target 36 bp. Equity $10,000 at 0.5% risk = $50. One basket lot loses $240 at the stop plus about $12 of entry cost: 50 ÷ 252 → 0.19 × $100k. Risk at the stop ≈ $48 (0.48% of equity).

Line in the Trade viewWhat it tells you
ReplicationThe smallest size at which every leg is at or above its minimum lot and each currency within ±2% of notional of the exact index. Below it, rounding breaks the replication.
SummaryNotional and leverage (red above half the lowest leverage allowed on the legs), margin, entry cost (red above 15% of the stop), risk at the stop (or “unlimited” with no stop).
Pre-trade flagsNo stop · risk > 2% · stop under 0.35 × a day’s move · cost > 15% of the stop · high-impact news soon · revenge trade · same basket already open · same bet as single positions · tilt. Some open the Coach’s pre-mortem before sending.
How a basket executes

The legs are sent one by one, each at its own price: slippage on every leg comes on top of the risk shown. If one leg is not filled the others are closed again, paying their spreads. Each leg carries a wide disaster stop (3× the basket stop). On a local account the basket stop works only while this tab is open; with the tab closed or in the background, only those disaster stops protect the legs. On a live account the execution server manages the group.

Open baskets show P&L, the index move and the replication gap, with Edit, BE, ½ and Close. Closing one leg outside the basket splits the group. Below: your currency exposure, Chart my exposure as a basket, and stress tests (USD +1%, Risk-off, Risk-on, EUR −1%, Worst day).

Judge on the index

Judge the trade on the index chart, not on each pair: that is the price your stop and target live on.

Try the proxy first

A proxy that follows most of the index for a fraction of the spread is often the better trade.

Find the disguise

Compare with the 8 currency indices: a basket that tracks one of them closely is that currency in disguise.
