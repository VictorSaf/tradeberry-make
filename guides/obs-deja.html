01

The question

The question

“Has the last stretch of this pair happened before? The closest shapes in the history of all 16 pairs, what followed them, and whether that ever predicted anything here.”

02

What you see

Hover the chart: the nearest match path lights up with its pair, time, shape distance and result; click to keep it selected. Hover the current pattern for its own move. In the table under the chart, the ↗ button on a match opens it on the Trade chart (that pair, that timeframe, scrolled to the moment of the match); a ← back to Déjà vu button on the chart brings you back.

03

Controls

ControlOptions · defaultEffect
Bar5m · 15m · 1HThe bar the pattern and its look-ahead are measured on. Larger bars cover more time with the same number of bars.
pattern16 · 32 · 64 barsThe shape compared: the last N bars (the duration is shown next to it). Shorter finds more matches but each says less.
look ahead6 · 12 · 24 barsWhat the matches did over the next N bars: use the horizon you would hold the trade for.

3 × 3 × 3 = 27 settings per pair, which is why the verdict is widened for 27 (see Statistics in one page, Fig. 6 in the Observatory overview). Déjà vu redraws only when a new bar of its timeframe closes.

04

How it is computed

history up to 4,000 bars of the timeframe for each of the 16 pairs needs pattern + look-ahead + 40 bars
pattern log path of the last W bars, z-scored: shape only, not size
search every W-bar window on any pair whose look-ahead ended before the pattern started
distance Σ (difference of z-scores)² · keep the 12 closest · one per moment (ends ≥ H bars apart)
rescale each match’s moves × (our bar volatility ÷ theirs), in this pair’s pips at today’s price
baseline every 5th window of all pairs: a random window’s distance, and the no-match band (25–75%)
05

The verdict: has this ever predicted anything here?

At up to 40 earlier moments, one look-ahead apart, the same search runs on what was known then. Each test scores whether the sign of the matches’ median move was the sign of what followed.

ConditionVerdictMeaning
Fewer than 20 testsNot enough tests (N of 20)Read the matches as a description only. A shorter timeframe, pattern or look-ahead leaves more history to test on.
Wilson interval (z = 3.11) entirely above 50%Edge in this sample · x%The whole widened interval is above a coin flip. With 432 settings across the 16 pairs some still clear it by chance: check on new data before trusting it.
Interval entirely below 50%Worse than a coin flip · x%Wrong more often than right. On a random walk that is chance too, not a contrarian signal.
Interval contains 50%No edge on <pair> · x%No better than a coin flip here. The direction below is noise; the spread still shows how far price moved after similar shapes.

Up and down colours are used for the matches only when the verdict is an edge; otherwise the direction stays neutral. A second line checks the middle-half coverage: how often what followed landed inside the matches’ 25–75% band, against a target of 50% (under 50%: the band was narrower than the outcomes; over: wider). It checks the width, not the direction.

Read the grey band first

It is what “no information” looks like. If the matches’ band sits inside it, the shape added nothing.

Use the spread

Even with no edge, the matches show how far price moved after similar shapes: a reference for stop and target distance.

Check the distance

If the closest match is not much closer than a random window, the “matches” are not really similar.

Limits

A majority up is common by chance with 12 matches. Matches on correlated pairs at the same hour count once, but the 16 pairs still share currencies. An “Edge in this sample” is a hypothesis for new data, not a forecast.
