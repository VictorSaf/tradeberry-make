Challenge

A rehearsal room for prop-firm evaluations. Pick a set of rules (profit target, daily loss limit, max loss, minimum days, consistency), run a practice attempt on your own account under those rules, and see the odds that your real closed trades would pass or breach them, with and without an edge. It is built to answer one question honestly: should you pay for a real challenge yet?

I

Orientation

What the tab does, what to read first, where everything is.

01

What the tab is, and what it is not

A prop-firm challenge is a paid test: reach a profit target without breaking a loss limit, and the firm funds you. The Challenge tab copies the mechanics of such tests so you can practise them and measure yourself against them before paying.

Practice attempt

The rules run live on your account from the moment you press Start practice attempt. Equity is checked on every market update; touching a floor ends the attempt and closes every position.

Readiness odds

Your own closed trades, re-ordered at random 20,000 times against the same rules. The result is a pass / breach / neither split, as you traded and with your edge removed.

Discipline checks

Six checks on things you control (stops, size, tilt, daily breaches, practice). Any failing check gives Not ready; the best verdict is Borderline. The tab never says “Ready”.

Why the best verdict is only “Borderline”

A pass in a few weeks can come from discipline plus luck. The tab can check your discipline, but it cannot prove an edge from a few dozen trades, so it always shows you the odds with zero edge next to your own. A real challenge is a paid bet; the tab’s job is to tell you its price honestly.

Not real money, not advice

The practice attempt runs on your tradeBerry account; nothing is sent to any prop firm. The presets are generic readings of published rules, not any firm’s official terms. Nothing here is financial advice.

05

Profit target and phases

The profit target is a percentage of the initial capital: the balance the attempt started with. It is counted on closed balance with no open positions. Floating profit does not count: you reach a target by closing trades, and the phase completes only when you are flat.

A 2-step rule set has two phases. Each phase is a fresh account of the initial capital: phase 2 starts from the balance phase 1 ended with, but its target and limits are measured on the initial capital, and the profit of phase 1 does not count toward it. (Code notes cite FTMO and FundedNext as firms that structure phases this way; no endorsement.)

When the target is reached but something else is still missing (days, profitable days, consistency, or open positions), a note says Target reached — followed by what is still needed, and adds “Limits still apply until the phase is complete.” The limits keep counting: you can still breach after reaching the target.

06

The daily loss limit

The daily limit says how far equity (floating P&L and swap included) may fall during one trading day. Three settings define it:

SettingOptionsEffect
Daily loss %0.5–20%, or noneThe size of the allowed fall.
Daily measured oninitial capital · day startThe amount is a % of the initial capital (all presets), or of the day-start value (custom rules only).
Daily frombalance at day start · higher of balance and equityThe anchor the fall is measured from. With an open profit carried into the day, “higher of” sets the floor higher.
Day resets in / at hourEurope/Prague, Europe/London, America/New_York, America/Chicago, UTC · hour 0–23When a new trading day starts: the anchor is re-read and the daily warnings re-arm. Daylight saving is handled.
anchor = balance at day start (or max(balance, equity) at day start)
daily amount = daily% × initial capital (or daily% × the day-start value)
daily floor = anchor − daily amount
breach when equity ≤ daily floor
Worked example · 2-step · static drawdown, $100,000

Yesterday you closed at 103,000 and you are flat at midnight Prague time. Today’s anchor is 103,000 and the daily amount is 5% × 100,000 = 5,000, so the daily floor is 98,000. The max floor is still 90,000. The higher floor, 98,000, is the one that binds today.

Open positions count

The limit is measured on equity, not balance. A position showing −$3,000 that you intend to “let come back” uses $3,000 of the day’s room right now.

07

Max loss: static, end-of-day trailing, intraday trailing

The max loss limit bounds the fall over the whole phase. Its amount is always max% × the capital; what changes is where the floor sits:

ModeFloorWhen the floor movesStrictness
staticphase start − amountNever during the phase.Most forgiving: profit widens your room.
end-of-day trailinghigh-water − amountAt each day reset, the high-water mark rises to the balance (or equity, per Trails on) if it is higher.Profit kept overnight raises the floor.
intraday trailinghigh-water − amountOn every check. With Trails on: equity, an open profit raises the floor even if it is never closed.Strictest: a winner that comes back costs you room.

Floor stops at start balance (lock): once the trailing floor reaches the phase’s start balance it stops rising. From then on the max limit behaves like a static floor at the start balance. Both trailing presets use the lock.

The intraday trailing trap

On the Intraday trailing preset (6%), a trade that runs to +$4,000 floating and is closed at break-even leaves your floor $4,000 higher than before: your room shrank by $4,000 without a single loss. The tab applies the run-up before the drawdown inside a trade in its odds for the same reason (chapter 16).

08

Trading days, profitable days, consistency

These rules never breach an attempt. They only hold back the completion of a phase: until they are met, a reached target waits.

Consistency is not a breach

As the preset notes say: “Breaking the consistency rule is not a breach: you keep trading until the best day is ≤ 50% of the profit.” The catch: more trading means more exposure to the loss limits while you wait.

Profitable days on 2-step · profitable days: 3 days with a closed profit of at least 0.5% of the capital, i.e. $500 on $100,000. A +$450 day is a trading day, but not a profitable one.

Running the rules live on your account: start, checks, readouts, guard rails, endings.

10

Starting an attempt

The empty card says No practice attempt running and explains: it runs on your demo account from its balance at start, equity is checked on every market update, the daily limit resets at the preset’s hour, and nothing is real money.

On start, the balance becomes the initial capital, the high-water mark and the first day’s anchor. A toast confirms “Practice attempt started” with the rules’ name. If the market Speed is above 60×, it is capped at 60× for the attempt: rules are checked every 250 ms, and at 300× that would be 75 market seconds between checks, enough to miss a breach.

Where the rules run

On an account connected to the execution server, the attempt is started, stopped and evaluated by the server, not in your browser; the tab shows the state the server reports and the same messages. Your rule choice, custom rules and readiness options are saved on the server too.

An attempt starts flat by design

Positions opened before the start would carry unknown risk into a fresh account, so the tab refuses. Example trades loaded from Help (the demo history) also void a running attempt: “Attempt voided: the account was replaced (demo loaded or cleared).”

11

How an attempt is judged, step by step

The check runs on every market update, right after that update’s stops, targets and margin stop-outs have been processed. Each pass goes through the same steps:

Equity for limits

Floating P&L and swap included; the live price model has no commission on top.

Balance for targets

Closed balance only, and only when flat. An open winner is not a pass.

Days in the rules’ clock

Trading days, profitable days and the daily anchor all use the preset’s reset time zone and hour, not your computer’s midnight.

13

Guard rails on the ticket

During an attempt the order ticket knows your room. It adds three kinds of protection, based on the money at risk to each stop:

SituationWhat happensText
Risk of this order + risk already on open positions and pending orders ≥ roomBlockedWould breach the challenge daily limit at the stop
No stop lossWarning + pre-mortemChallenge: no stop loss — one trade can breach the attempt.
Risk ≥ 50% of the room leftWarning + pre-mortemChallenge: this trade risks 60% of the room left before the daily limit.
Within the cool-down after 80% of the daily roomPre-mortem on every tradeChallenge cool-down: … left after using 80% of today’s loss limit

“Risk already on” counts each open position from the current price to its stop (a stop in profit counts zero) and each pending order from its price to its stop. Positions and orders without a stop are not counted; the no-stop warning covers them. Baskets are not checked here. The cool-down lasts the tilt guard’s cool-down setting (15 minutes by default) and starts with the toast “Challenge: 80% of today’s loss limit used”.

What the block means for a breach

If every trade has a stop and fills at it, the ticket will not let a stop-out reach the floor: the trade whose stop would touch it is blocked. Breaches then come from what a stop does not cover: gaps over the weekend or a session close, slippage, swap, a trade without a stop, or several trades losing together beyond what was counted.

Try it: how many losing trades fit in today’s room

The same arithmetic as the ticket: room = equity − the higher floor; the next order is flagged when its risk is ≥ 50% of the room and blocked when it is ≥ the room. This assumes a fixed $ risk per trade, one trade at a time, every loss a full stop, flat at the day start, and no new high-water mark yet.

Daily used = (anchor − equity) ÷ daily amount; the toasts fire at 50% and 80% (80% also starts the cool-down). Blocked = risk ≥ room. Pre-mortem = risk ≥ 50% of room.

Worked example · defaults

2-step · static, $100,000, 1% = $1,000 a trade. Daily room $5,000. Trades 1–3 pass freely; after 3 losses the daily room is $2,000, so trade 4 risks 50% of it and gets a pre-mortem; after 4 losses (80% used, cool-down) the room is $1,000 and trade 5 is blocked. Lower the risk to 0.5% and nine losing trades fit.

The breach stop-out

A breach closes every open position at once, marked Stop-out (challenge breach). A position whose market is closed (session over, weekend, no quotes) cannot fill: it waits and closes at the first check after its market opens, at that price, gap included. The banner and a later toast (“Challenge stop-out completed”) say so. The log’s return is updated after those closes.

The Breach day list shows up to 12 trades closed that day, with the warnings each carried (for example “Within 10 min of a loss”, “No stop loss”), then “2 of 5 trades that day carried warnings.” The Coach insights show the same once: “Challenge breached on the daily limit — 2 of 5 trades that day carried warnings. See Challenge.”

A pass is not proof

The pass banner says it plainly: “A pass shows your risk control held on this path. It is not evidence of an edge.” Use a pass to confirm discipline; use the readiness odds for the price of the bet.

The attempt log

The last 20 finished attempts, newest first: Started, Rules, Result, Phase (the one it ended in), Days (trading days over all phases) and Return (final balance ÷ initial capital − 1, so a 2-step pass shows both phases together). The log is saved with your account, and Export / Import in Help carries it.

Worked example · a phase 1 in numbers

2-step · static, $100,000. Day 1 closes +1,500 (balance 101,500; that day’s floor 95,000). Day 2 −2,000 (99,500; floor 96,500). Day 3 +4,000 (103,500; floor 94,500). Day 4 +3,000 (106,500; floor 98,500). Day 5 +3,600 → 110,100, flat, 5 trading days ≥ 4: “Phase 1 passed in 5 trading days”. Phase 2 starts at 110,100 with target 115,100, max floor 100,100 and a daily amount of 5,000.

IV

Readiness odds

What your own closed trades say about these rules, how the odds are computed, and how to use them.

15

Which trades are used

The readiness card works on your own closed trades in the history. Each closed row becomes a return: the money it made or lost as a share of the balance before it. Then the rows are grouped into trading days with the selected rules’ reset.

Why a what-if risk matters

As traded asks: would my trades, sized as I sized them, survive these rules? A fixed risk such as 0.5% asks: would the same trades, each sized to lose 0.5% at its stop, survive? It separates your trade selection from your position sizing.

16

20,000 random attempts

The odds come from a day-block bootstrap: your trading days are drawn at random, with replacement, and played one after another against the rules, many times. Whole days are kept together, so a bad afternoon of revenge trades stays a bad afternoon instead of being spread out.

One random attempt

  1. Equity starts at 1 (the initial capital). Each phase has up to Horizon trading days (20, 60 or 120); a 2-step can use up to twice the horizon.
  2. Each day: draw one of your days at random. The account is flat at the day start, so the daily floor is that day’s start − the daily amount.
  3. Each trade of that day, in order: first the run-up (MFE) raises an intraday trailing floor on equity, then the drawdown (MAE) is tested against both floors, then the return is applied and tested again. Run-up before drawdown is the conservative order for a trailing floor.
  4. Touching a floor ends the attempt as a daily or max breach.
  5. At day end: count profitable days and the best day; end-of-day trailing raises its high-water mark. If the target, min days, profitable days and consistency are all met, the phase passes; the next phase is a fresh account at 1.
  6. A phase that runs out of horizon days ends the attempt as neither.

Zero edge

The second scenario subtracts your average return from every trade (the Method note says by how many basis points), so the average trade makes exactly zero. Your sizing, your spread of results, your bad days and your drawdowns inside trades all stay; only the edge goes. It answers: if my trade selection is worth nothing, how often do these rules pass me by luck, given how I size and how I lose?

Why zero edge is the row to decide with

A few dozen trades cannot tell a real edge from a lucky run. The As traded row assumes your past average continues; the With zero edge row does not. The card’s fixed line: “These odds describe your sizing and rule discipline with no edge assumed, not your chances on a live account.”

The run is deterministic: the random seed comes from your trades, the rules and the options, so the same inputs give the same numbers (the seed is shown in Method). It re-runs only when one of them changes, and the card shows “Running 20,000 random attempts… 40%” meanwhile.

What the defaults show

At 1% risk, 3 trades a day, 2-step · static gives about 37% at zero edge, roughly 3 attempts per pass. Switch to 1-step · end-of-day trailing: its 3% daily limit is nearly three full losses, so the daily breach share jumps and check D5 fails. Raise the risk to 2% on 2-step · static: three losses in a day exceed 5% and daily breaches dominate.

Fixed % of a growing balance

Returns compound: a 1% risk on a balance of 108,000 is $1,080, while the daily limit stays $3,000 or $5,000 of the initial capital. As the account grows, the same % risk uses more of a fixed daily limit. This is in the tab’s model too.

Before you pay for a real challenge

The tab is built around one decision. A routine that uses every part of it:

  1. Rules
    Model the exact challengePick the closest preset, or copy the firm’s current page into + Use my firm’s current rules…. Check the day reset time zone.
  2. Data
    30 trades over 10 days, at leastYour own closed trades, with stops, at the size you mean to use.
  3. Checks
    Clear every “Not ready” reasonStops on ≥ 95% of trades, size within half the daily limit, few tilt trades, no day past the limit, daily odds ≤ 10%.
  4. Sizing
    Try the what-if risksCompare As traded with 0.25% / 0.5% / 1%. Pick the size whose zero-edge daily breach share is small.
  5. Practice
    Run a practice attemptUntil it passes, or runs 10 trading days without a breach (check D6).
  6. Price
    Read the zero-edge line“About N paid attempts per pass” × the fee is what a pass costs you if you have no edge.
Worked example · the price of a pass

Borderline, zero-edge pass 25% (90% interval 15%–36%). That is about 4 paid attempts per pass. At a $500 fee that is about $2,000 of fees per pass at zero edge, and the interval says it could be 3 to 7 attempts. If you do have an edge, the As traded row is your upside; you have not proven it yet.

Limits and caveats

Generic presets

Readings of published rules at a date, with notes where the text was ambiguous. Not any firm’s terms.

Trades one at a time

The odds play your trades in sequence; overlapping positions are not modelled, and days are independent of each other.

Drawdown inside trades

Measured on checks 250 ms apart; older trades without it understate breach odds (the card says so).

Your past, resampled

The model only re-orders days you already had. A kind of day you never had cannot appear.

Gaps

A position on a closed market is closed at its next open after a breach, at that price; a gap counts against or for it.

Not advice

The odds describe sizing and discipline. They are not a forecast or financial advice.

Glossary

Prop-firm challenge
A paid evaluation: reach a profit target within loss limits to be offered a funded account.
Initial capital
The balance at the start of the attempt; every % in the rules applies to it.
Phase
One stage of the evaluation; each later phase is a fresh account of the initial capital.
Equity
Balance plus floating P&L and swap of open positions. The limits use it.
Closed balance
Balance from closed trades only. The target uses it, with no open positions.
Anchor
The day-start value the daily limit is measured from: the balance, or the higher of balance and equity.
Floor
The equity level that ends the attempt when touched.
Room
Equity minus the higher floor: the loss you can still take.
High-water mark
The highest balance or equity a trailing floor has followed so far.
Static / trailing
A floor fixed for the phase, or one that rises with the high-water mark (at day end or on every check).
Lock
“Floor stops at start balance”: a trailing floor stops rising there.
Trading day
A day, in the rules’ reset, with at least one fill.
Consistency
A cap on the best day’s share of total profit; not a breach.
MAE / MFE
The worst and best floating P&L reached during a trade.
Bootstrap
Resampling your own data with replacement to see how much a result depends on chance.
Zero edge
Your trades with their average return removed, so the average trade makes nothing.
90% interval
The 5th to 95th percentile of the pass rate across the 200 resamples.
Pre-mortem
The prompt before a flagged trade that asks you to imagine it lost and say why.
